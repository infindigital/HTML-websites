Ship Repair Services in Sri Lanka: A Practical Guide for Owners and Operators

7 min read · Published 2026-02-04

Sitting astride the main east–west trade route across the Indian Ocean, Sri Lanka sees a constant stream of tankers, container ships, bulk carriers and offshore units passing its coast or calling at its ports. Colombo is one of South Asia’s leading transhipment hubs, while Galle, Hambantota and Trincomalee extend berthing and anchorage options around the island. For owners and operators, dependable, class-compliant ship repair capability at these ports is a commercial necessity rather than a convenience.

This guide sets out what to plan for when arranging repairs at Sri Lankan ports, from routine afloat maintenance to survey-driven steel and machinery work.

Afloat repairs versus dry-docking

Most in-service defects can be handled afloat, alongside a berth or at anchorage, without lifting the vessel out of the water. Pump overhauls, cooler cleaning and pressure testing, valve and pipework renewals, and running-gear repairs are all routinely completed within a normal port call. ARCSHIP’s marine mechanical solutions and engine overhauling teams are structured for exactly this kind of fast, alongside intervention while a vessel is on the berth or riding at the Colombo anchorage.

Dry-docking becomes necessary when the work reaches the underwater hull, tail shaft, rudder or sea valves, or when class calls for a docking survey. Even where the docking itself takes place elsewhere in the region, much of the preparation, staging and post-dock commissioning can be organised locally to compress the overall schedule and reduce time out of trade.

Class and flag drive the scope

The single biggest influence on a repair scope is the vessel’s survey position. Classification societies, Lloyd’s Register, Bureau Veritas, ABS, Korean Register, RINA, ClassNK, the Indian Register of Shipping, CCS and Tasneef among them, set the inspection and testing regime, and the flag state layers its own statutory requirements on top. Planning repairs around the survey calendar avoids duplicated attendances and keeps certificates valid.

Repairs planned around the class survey calendar cost less and cause less off-hire than defects addressed reactively.

Planning a repair port call

  • Advise scope early. The earlier a service provider has the scope, the more spares and test equipment can be pre-positioned before the vessel arrives.
  • Confirm access and permits. Hot work, enclosed-space entry and over-side work all require permits and, frequently, port and terminal approvals at the calling port.
  • Align with safety servicing. A repair call is the natural moment to complete life-saving appliance and fire-fighting appliance servicing that would otherwise demand a separate attendance.
  • Document everything. Condition reports, test records and class certificates protect the vessel’s trading status and its resale value.

Why a single technical partner helps

Coordinating separate contractors for steel, machinery, safety equipment and calibration adds interface risk and lengthens the critical path. Bringing the work under one QHSE system, with a single point of accountability, reduces that risk and makes the schedule easier to hold within the laycan, particularly on a fast transhipment call at Colombo.
