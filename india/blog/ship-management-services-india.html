Ship Management Services in India: What Full Technical Management Covers

7 min read · Published 2026-04-18

Ship management is the discipline of running a vessel on the owner’s behalf so that it trades safely, in full compliance and profitably. Instead of carrying technical, crewing and compliance departments in-house, an owner or charterer appoints a single accountable manager who takes responsibility for the ship under the ISM Code and reports against agreed budgets and performance measures.

For tonnage calling at Indian ports such as Mumbai, JNPT (Nhava Sheva), Cochin (Kochi), New Mangalore, Deendayal (Kandla), Chennai, Visakhapatnam, Paradip and Port Blair, a manager who understands Directorate General of Shipping (DG Shipping) expectations and Mercantile Marine Department (MMD) survey routines keeps calls predictable and off-hire low. India’s long coastline, spread across the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal, means a vessel may load on one coast and discharge on the other within a single voyage, so the technical and commercial functions have to work as one.

What full technical management covers

ARCSHIP structures ship management around the functions a vessel needs to keep trading, delivered as either full management or a technical-only mandate:

  • Planned maintenance: a computerised maintenance system, defect rectification and condition monitoring that protect reliability and asset value, backed by in-house engine overhauling and mechanical support.
  • Crewing: recruitment, certification and welfare of competent, correctly certificated seafarers, with manning that meets flag, STCW and MLC 2006 requirements.
  • Compliance: ISM and ISPS implementation, class and statutory survey management, and MMD and port State control inspection readiness.
  • Purchasing: spares, stores, lubricants and provisions procured against budget, with the logistics to land them at the right Indian port on time.
  • Budgeting: annual operating budgets, cost control and transparent monthly reporting so owners see where money is spent.
  • Dry-dock management: specification, yard selection, tendering and supervision of the docking, including class-driven renewal work.
  • Commercial support: voyage and port-call coordination, bunkering and the operational interface that keeps the vessel earning.

The ISM and QHSE framework

Every credible manager runs on a documented safety management system built around the ISM Code and the wider SOLAS, MARPOL and MLC 2006 regime. The system defines shipboard procedures, incident and near-miss reporting, internal audits and the Designated Person Ashore who links vessel and office. ARCSHIP operates ISO 9001, 14001 and 45001 certified management systems, so quality, environmental and occupational-health controls are audited rather than assumed.

In practice this framework is what an MMD surveyor, a class attending surveyor or a port State control officer at an Indian port tests during an inspection. Clean records for maintenance, drills, oil-record books and certificates are the difference between a routine attendance and a detention. The manager also runs the audit cycle that keeps the ship’s Safety Management Certificate and the company’s Document of Compliance valid, closing internal-audit findings before an external verification falls due.

Good management is measured in avoided off-hire, clean inspections and predictable operating cost.

Classification and statutory survey management

A managed vessel keeps class with a recognised society and holds its statutory certificates current at all times. A capable manager tracks survey windows, arranges attendance and closes conditions of class before they fall due. ARCSHIP works with recognised classification societies including the Indian Register of Shipping (IRS), Lloyd’s Register, Bureau Veritas, ABS, RINA, ClassNK, Korean Register, CCS and Tasneef, and coordinates with DG Shipping and MMD for flag and statutory matters.

Where a ship needs hands-on safety and engineering work between surveys, the manager can draw on in-house life-saving appliance and fire-fighting appliance servicing so certificates stay valid without waiting on multiple sub-contractors.

Why owners use a single accountable manager

Owners and charterers with vessels trading to Indian ports appoint one manager because a single accountable party removes the gaps that open up when technical, crewing and compliance work is split across vendors. When a defect arises during a call at an Indian port, one team owns the response, the reporting and the cost. Points to weigh when choosing a manager:

  • Certified management systems (ISO 9001, 14001 and 45001) and a clear QHSE culture
  • Transparent reporting on maintenance, budget and inspection status
  • In-house engineering rather than fully sub-contracted technical work
  • Recognised working relationships with classification societies, DG Shipping and MMD
  • A local footprint at the ports a vessel actually calls

With group offices in Navi Mumbai, Kochi, Port Blair and Udupi, ARCSHIP can attend vessels across India’s west and east coasts and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands from a team that already knows the local ports and surveyors.
