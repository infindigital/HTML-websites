Marine Safety Equipment Services in India: LSA and FFA Explained

8 min read · Published 2026-03-11

Every SOLAS vessel carries two categories of survival equipment that are surveyed and serviced on a fixed cycle: life-saving appliances (LSA) and fire-fighting appliances (FFA). Both must be maintained by competent, approved service stations, and both are scrutinised during Mercantile Marine Department (MMD) surveys and port-state control checks. For vessels calling at Mumbai, JNPT (Nhava Sheva), Kochi, Chennai, Visakhapatnam and India's other major ports, keeping this equipment in date is central to trading without detention.

India's flag-state and port-state framework sits under the Directorate General of Shipping (DG Shipping), with statutory surveys carried out through the MMD and class maintained by societies such as the Indian Register of Shipping (IRS), Lloyd's Register, Bureau Veritas, ABS and others. Servicing carried out to SOLAS, the LSA Code, the FSS Code and, where relevant, MED (Marine Equipment Directive) approval is what an inspector expects to see reflected in the vessel's certificates and records.

What life-saving appliance servicing covers

LSA servicing spans the equipment a crew relies on to abandon ship and survive: lifeboats and rescue boats, davits and launching appliances, liferafts, lifebuoys and lifejackets, immersion and anti-exposure suits, and pyrotechnics. Servicing includes annual inspection, five-yearly load testing of launching appliances and boats, and re-certification of inflatable equipment. ARCSHIP's life-saving appliance servicing is delivered to SOLAS and the LSA Code, and only SOLAS-approved marine safety products are fitted as replacements.

Load testing and davits

Launching appliances, winches and on-load release gear must be examined and tested at defined intervals. Missing a davit load test is a common detainable deficiency at Indian ports, and it cannot be resolved without a competent service attendance and the corresponding test certificate.

What fire-fighting appliance servicing covers

FFA servicing keeps the vessel's fixed and portable firefighting systems ready to operate: fixed CO2 and foam systems, water-mist and sprinkler systems, fixed detection and alarm systems, portable and wheeled extinguishers, firefighter's outfits and breathing apparatus. Work includes refilling and hydrostatic testing of extinguishers and cylinders, pressure testing of fire mains and hoses, and functional testing of detection under the FSS Code. See fire-fighting appliance servicing for the full scope.

An out-of-date extinguisher or an untested breathing-apparatus cylinder is enough to hold a vessel at berth during an Indian port-state control inspection.

MMD survey and port-state control at Indian ports

Statutory LSA and FFA surveys are tied to the vessel's Safety Equipment Certificate, and Indian MMD offices survey against the same SOLAS intervals worldwide inspectors apply. Where gauges, alarms and detection heads form part of the safety chain, in-date calibration records should be available alongside the service reports. A typical port-call attendance at Mumbai, Kandla (Deendayal), New Mangalore, Paradip or Port Blair covers:

  • Annual inspection and function testing of LSA and FFA equipment
  • Refilling, hydrotesting and re-certification of extinguishers and BA cylinders
  • Load testing of davits, winches and survival craft where due
  • Servicing and re-packing of inflatable liferafts and lifejackets
  • Issue of test certificates and a consolidated service report for the survey file

Keeping the vessel inspection-ready

The most reliable way to avoid a deficiency is to map every LSA and FFA due-date against the vessel's trading pattern and to schedule the attendance into a planned call at an Indian port. That turns safety servicing from a reactive scramble into a routine, documented event, timed to fit a container call at JNPT or a bunkering and cargo stop at Visakhapatnam. Keeping certificates, calibration slips and service reports filed and current means a boarding surveyor finds a complete, in-date record rather than an open item.
